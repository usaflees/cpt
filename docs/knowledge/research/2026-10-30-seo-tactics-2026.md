# Evidence-based SEO tactics (2025 to late 2026) for a small crypto price-tracking site (cryptopriceticker.com)

Research date: 2026-10-30. Primary sources are Google Search Central docs, blog posts and changelog, the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (Sept 11, 2025 version), Bing Webmaster docs and blog, and Cloudflare docs. Independent reporting comes from Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal and SE Roundtable. Studies come from Ahrefs, Seer and SISTRIX (as reported). Agency or SaaS case studies are flagged as low-confidence where mentioned. Anything dated before 2025 is marked "(pre-2025, possibly stale)".

## 1. Google Search Central changes in 2025 to 2026 that matter here (updates, AI features, title links, structured data, JS SEO, Core Web Vitals)

### Takeaway
Google says plainly that AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special optimization. You do not need llms.txt, special schema or "chunking". The same indexing and snippet eligibility rules apply. Several rich results were removed in 2025 and 2026. The most relevant removal here is the FAQ rich result, gone from May 7, 2026. Rendering rules are unchanged: links must be `<a href>`, the canonical belongs in the initial HTML, and server-side or pre-rendering is still recommended. The Core Web Vitals targets are LCP ≤2.5s, INP <200ms and CLS <0.1.

### Cited Findings
**Ranking and spam updates (official dashboard)**
- 2025 confirmed updates: March 2025 core (Mar 13, ~14 days), June 2025 core (Jun 30, ~17 days), August 2025 spam (Aug 26, ~27 days), December 2025 core (Dec 11, ~18 days). — [Google Search Status Dashboard, Ranking history](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)
- 2026 confirmed updates: February 2026 Discover update (Feb 5), March 2026 spam (Mar 24), March 2026 core (Mar 27, ~12 days), May 2026 core (May 21, ~12 days), June 2026 spam (Jun 24), August 2026 spam (Aug 18), September 2026 spam (Sep 24, ~14 days). — [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)
- December 2025 changelog: Google "added a section on smaller core updates" to its documentation. — [Google Search documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- May 2026: Google clarified that its spam policies also apply to generative AI responses in Google Search. Spam is now defined to include "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search". — [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- April 2026: Google added "back button hijacking" to the malicious practices spam policy, and clarified that spam reports may be used for manual actions. — [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

**AI Overviews and AI Mode: what Google says**
- "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." To be eligible as a supporting link, a page "must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (updated 2025-12-10)
- "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- AI Overviews and AI Mode use "query fan-out": they issue several related searches across subtopics. AI Overviews "are only shown when our systems determine that it is additive to classic Search, and as such, often don't trigger." — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- The controls are `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet` and `noindex`. Traffic from AI features is counted in the Search Console Performance report under the "Web" search type. — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- In May 2026 Google added a guide on optimizing for generative AI features. In June 2026 it added a note on llms.txt. The guide says: "LLMS.txt files and other 'special' markup: You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as Google Search itself doesn't use them ... Doing so will neither harm nor help." — [Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- The same guide treats these as myths: "chunking" content, rewriting content just for AI, and over-focusing on structured data ("Structured data isn't required for generative AI search"). It says "AEO"/"GEO" is still SEO, and you "can ignore tactics like 'chunking' content, creating unnecessary AI text files (like llms.txt), or pursuing inauthentic mentions." — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- The guide's main advice is "valuable, non-commodity content". It contrasts commodity content (common knowledge) with content that adds unique first-hand or expert insight. It also warns that creating separate content for every query variation or fan-out query "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses ... violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy." — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- Search Console has a new "Search generative AI" control under Settings. It decides whether a site's links and content are included in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Inclusion is the default. Excluding removes the site's impressions and traffic from those features. The control doesn't affect training, which is governed by Google-Extended. — [Search generative AI control help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024) (updated 2026-08-31)
- A "Generative AI performance report" in Search Console (announced June 2026) shows impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, device and country. — [Gen AI performance report help (PT)](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139); [Search Central blog index listing "Introducing Search Generative AI performance reports" (June 2026)](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/search-console-branded-filter?hl=en)
- Google's May 2025 "succeeding in AI search" post says to focus on unique, satisfying content, make sure structured data matches visible content, and support text with high-quality images and video. — [Google blog, May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search)

**Title links**
- Best practices: every page should have a descriptive, concise `<title>`. Avoid keyword stuffing. Avoid "repeated or boilerplate text", including long titles that vary by a single piece of information. Brand titles concisely, with the site name at the start or end after a delimiter. Make the main title the most prominent text on the page, for example the first `<h1>`. — [Influencing title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (updated 2025-12-10)
- Sources Google uses to build the title link: `<title>`, the main visual title, headings/`<h1>`, `og:title`, prominent styled text, other page text, anchor text on the page and in inbound links, and WebSite structured data (for site names). Updates "may take a few days to a few weeks" to show. — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
- Google says "avoid including flight price information in `<title>` elements" because prices change so fast that the title link may not match the page. This is the closest official analogue to live crypto prices (the flight guidance dates from Nov 2023, pre-2025, and is still in the doc). — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
- For domain-level site names, Google "may omit the site name from the title link, if it's repetitive with the site name that's already shown in the search result." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)

**Meta descriptions**
- For large database-driven sites, "programmatic generation of the descriptions can be appropriate and are encouraged. Good descriptions are human-readable and diverse. Page-specific data is a good candidate for programmatic generation." There's no length limit; truncation depends on device width. — [Control your snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (updated 2026-04-20)

**Structured data: deprecations, and what still works**
- June 12, 2025: Google began phasing out Book Actions, Course Info, ClaimReview, Estimated Salary, Learning Video, Special Announcement and Vehicle Listing. "This update won't affect how pages are ranked." Reporting for six of these was removed on Sept 9, 2025. — [Simplifying the search results page](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results); [SE Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-drops-reporting-on-several-structured-data-types-461744)
- Nov 2025: Practice problem markup was deprecated (removed Jan 2026). Dataset markup was clarified as used only by Dataset Search, not Google Search. The Book Actions deprecation was reversed. — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-deprecates-practice-problem-structured-data-in-search/560076); [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- **FAQ rich result:** "This feature will no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026." The documentation was later removed. (Since Aug 2023 it had been limited to authoritative government and health sites.) — [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [2023 HowTo/FAQ changes (pre-2025)](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)
- Google says "Structured data that's not being used does not cause problems for Search, but also has no visible effects in Google Search." (pre-2025 statement) — [Google blog 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)
- WebSite structured data is still a listed input for site names. — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
- Google points to a Jan 2025 post on "simplifying breadcrumbs" as part of the same effort. — [Simplifying the search results page](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results) (I did not fetch that post; see Gaps)

**JavaScript SEO**
- Google renders with evergreen Chromium. Every 200-status page goes into a render queue that "may stay on this queue for a few seconds, but it can take longer". "Server-side or pre-rendering is still a great idea because it makes your website faster for users and crawlers, and not all bots can run JavaScript." — [JavaScript SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics) (updated 2026-03-04)
- "Google can only discover your links if they are `<a>` HTML elements with an `href` attribute." JS-injected links are fine if they use that markup. `routerLink`, `onclick` and `javascript:` hrefs are not recommended. — [JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics); [Link best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable)
- Canonical: setting it in HTML is best, and JS should not change it to a different value. Noindex: if the original HTML has `noindex`, Google may skip rendering, so removing it with JS may not work. Dec 2025 changelog: Google clarified JS execution on non-200 pages, canonicalization for JS, and noindex with JS. — [JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics); [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- WRS "caches aggressively" and may ignore caching headers. Use content-fingerprinted filenames such as `main.2bb85551.js`. — [JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)
- Feb 2026: Google clarified Googlebot's default file size limits. — [Documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) (I did not fetch the details)

**Page experience and Core Web Vitals**
- The targets are LCP within 2.5s, INP under 200ms and CLS under 0.1. "We highly recommend site owners achieve good Core Web Vitals for success with Search ... This ... aligns with what our core ranking systems seek to reward." — [Core Web Vitals doc](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) (updated 2025-12-10)
- Google says not to focus on "only one or two aspects of page experience". — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

### Inferences
- **llms.txt**: don't build it for Google, which ignores it. It is harmless if added for other agents, but low priority.
- **FAQPage JSON-LD**: adding it will no longer produce a rich result, since that feature ended May 7, 2026. Keep the `<details>` FAQ for users, and only add FAQPage markup if it exactly mirrors visible text and you want it for non-Google consumers.
- **Prices in titles and meta descriptions**: by analogy with the flight guidance, live prices are likely to be ignored or rewritten in title links. Keep live prices out of `<title>`. A build-time "as of" snapshot in the meta description is defensible only if it matches the page, and it will go stale between crawls.
- **The live table depends on WebSockets.** Google's render is a one-off headless snapshot, so a persistent WebSocket stream should not be expected to populate. Inferred from how WRS is described; Google does not document WebSocket handling. Everything Google should index (stats, about text, internal links) must be in the build-time HTML.
- **INP risk**: the main INP risk on a ticker page is long tasks from frequent DOM updates driven by WebSocket ticks. Batch updates with requestAnimationFrame or a throttle (for example ≤4 Hz). Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` and fixed-width cells so changing digits don't shift layout (CLS).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Jan 2025 "simplifying breadcrumbs" post, so I can't confirm the exact current display of breadcrumbs on mobile versus desktop. I believe Google stopped showing breadcrumb paths in mobile results, but this is unverified here. BreadcrumbList is not listed as deprecated in anything I read.
- I found no Google statement on how WRS treats WebSocket connections or long-lived timers.
- I did not fetch the detailed contents of the Dec 2025 "smaller core updates" section or the Feb 2026 file size limits.

## 2. YMYL / finance: how the Quality Rater Guidelines treat financial and crypto info pages; legitimate E-E-A-T signals for a small site

### Takeaway
The current QRG is dated Sept 11, 2025. It updated the YMYL definitions and defines "YMYL Financial Security" as topics that "could damage a person's ability to support themselves". A pure price tracker sits somewhere on the YMYL spectrum. Prices inform financial decisions, so trust matters. The biggest legitimate gains for a small site are transparency about who runs it and how the data is produced, plus contact information, accurate timestamps and no fake authors. Raters assess pages; they don't set rankings directly.

### Cited Findings
- QRG change log: **September 2025**, "Updated YMYL definitions; added additional examples". **January 2025**, "Updated Page Quality Lowest and Low sections to better align with Google Search Web Spam Policies". — [QRG PDF (Sept 11, 2025)](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Google called Sept 2025 "a minor update ... small clarifications and a handful of new examples. This update makes no change to our rating guidance." It added AI Overview examples. — [Search Engine Land, Sept 11, 2025](https://searchengineland.com/google-updates-search-quality-raters-guidelines-adding-ai-overview-examples-ymyl-definitions-461908); [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-quality-raters-guidelines-update-40092.html)
- YMYL definition: "YMYL Financial Security: Topics that could damage a person's ability to support themselves and their families." YMYL is "a spectrum ... clear YMYL, definitely not YMYL or something in between. Pages on clear YMYL topics require the most scrutiny." — [QRG](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Accuracy: "For pages on YMYL topics, consider the extent to which the content is accurate and consistent with well-established expert consensus." — [QRG §3.0](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- "We expect most websites to have some information about who ... is responsible for the website and who created the MC and some contact information, unless there is a good reason for anonymity." — [QRG](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- "Pages on YMYL topics and other pages that require a high level of user trust should receive a Low rating if there is an unsatisfying amount of information about who is responsible for the website or who created the content." Also: "A complete lack of information about who is responsible for the website and its content for YMYL pages" leads toward Lowest. — [QRG §4–5](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- A "Low: Financial" example: superficial stock-picking advice with "mostly commonly known information" and "no evidence that the author has financial expertise". — [QRG §5.7](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Lowest: "A webpage or website with 'fake' owner or content creator profiles", for example "AI generated content with made up 'author' profiles". — [QRG §4](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf). Google's helpful-content doc says the same: "Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials ...) is a form of deception." — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) (updated 2026-10-05)
- Google's self-assessment asks: "Does the content present information in a way that makes you want to trust it, such as clear sourcing, evidence of the expertise involved, background about the author or the site that publishes it, such as through links to an author page or a site's About page?" — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- "While E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor ... our systems give even more weight to content that aligns with strong E-E-A-T for topics that could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people ... 'Your Money or Your Life' topics." Trust is the most important component. — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Rater data "is not used directly in our ranking algorithms." — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- On the "How" of content: when automation is used, Google suggests disclosing it, explaining how it was used, and explaining why it was useful. — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Industry model: CoinMarketCap publishes a methodology and support pages explaining that price is "the volume weighted average of all market pair prices", how outliers are excluded, and that exchanges are queried every minute. — [CMC: How are prices calculated](https://support.coinmarketcap.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015968632-How-are-prices-calculated-on-CoinMarketCap); [CMC Market Data](https://support.coinmarketcap.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034116491-Market-Data)
- Kraken price pages carry a "Disclaimer: These materials are for general information purposes only and are not investment advice". — [Kraken BTC page](https://www.kraken.com/prices/bitcoin)

### Inferences
- E-E-A-T signals a small, honest site can legitimately add:
  - An About page naming the real person or entity responsible.
  - A Contact page or email.
  - A Methodology / Data Sources page: which exchanges and WebSocket feeds, how the displayed price is chosen or aggregated, the quote currency, update latency, outlier and stale-feed handling, what happens when a feed is down, and where supply data comes from.
  - A site-wide "not financial advice" disclaimer.
  - Visible "Data as of …" timestamps on the build-time snapshot.
  - A changelog or corrections note.
  - A short note on how the "About" paragraphs were written or reviewed, especially if AI drafted them.
- Link the Methodology page from every coin page's "Where this price comes from" paragraph. This turns templated boilerplate into a sourced claim.
- Don't invent an "analyst" byline. An Organization author pointing to the About page is the honest choice.

### Gaps
- The QRG doesn't name cryptocurrency price trackers specifically, as far as the excerpts showed. I didn't extract the full text of the Sept 2025 YMYL definition changes.
- There is no official statement on whether a pure live-price page counts as "clear YMYL" or "in between". This is a judgment call.

## 3. Programmatic/templated pages: acceptable versus "scaled content abuse"; minimum unique value; patterns for ticker sites

### Takeaway
Google's line is about purpose and added value, not the method of creation. Many pages made "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users" count as abuse "no matter how it's created". Templated pages are fine when each page carries data or functionality a user actually wants. Mueller (Sept 2026) warns that low-value programmatic pages can drag on a whole site for a long time. For a ticker site, the defensible pattern is per-coin computed data that is unique or hard to find (the site's own multi-exchange feed), rendered in HTML, with real navigation. The "About" paragraph alone is not enough.

### Cited Findings
- Scaled content abuse, defined: "when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users ... large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created." Examples include using generative AI "to generate many pages without adding value", "scraping feeds ... to generate many pages", and "stitching or combining content from different web pages without adding value". — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) (updated 2026-08-28)
- Doorway abuse includes "Creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy." — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Keyword stuffing explicitly includes "filling a web page with keywords **or numbers**". — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- QRG §4.6.5–4.6.6: content "created at scale with no original content or added value for users, should be rated Lowest, no matter how they are created". Lowest also applies if almost all main content is "copied, paraphrased, embedded, auto or AI generated, or reposted ... with little to no effort ... even if the page assigns credit". — [QRG](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Google defines "Effort" as including human work on "the content **or the systems powering it**". It names "building a custom interactive map" as high effort, and "automatically generating pages from feeds" without oversight as low effort. Also: "Attribution or giving credit to other sources doesn't replace the need for original effort." "Originality" means information "that aren't already available on other websites." — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- "Interactive features: Built-in functionality such as calculators, online tools" count as main content. — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Mueller (Sept 2026): "Programmatic SEO like this often leads to a site that's either spam, borderline spam, or low quality. It's easy to spin something up with many pages, it's hard to provide real value to users." He also said old low-value pages may keep weighing on the site, and recovery "tends to take time & significant effort". He didn't say all programmatic SEO is spam. — [SEJ, Sept 8, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-old-low-value-pages-may-affect-site-recovery/588837)
- Mueller (2023, pre-2025): "Programmatic SEO is often a fancy banner for spam." — [quoted in Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/programmatic-seo/)
- Google's spam policy says Google "generally applies a presumption that individual pages ... match the overall quality of other pages on the domain" (site reputation context). On July 2026 Search Off the Record, Mueller said that with strong site quality concerns, "we'll probably crawl a lot less. We'll index a lot less." — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [seo.com quoting SOTR](https://www.seo.com/basics/how-search-engines-work/indexing) (secondary)
- June 2025: Google issued manual actions for "large-scale content abuse". The August 2025 spam update continued enforcement. — [SEJ, Mar 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youre-not-scaling-content-youre-scaling-disappointment/569235) (reporting; not a primary Google statement)
- Generative AI guidance: "using generative AI tools ... to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse." You must fact-check AI output, including "`<title>` elements, meta description elements, structured data, and alternate texts." — [Using gen AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) (updated 2026-10-01)
- Low-confidence (agency marketing, not independently verified): one agency's tracking of 1,842 programmatic pages through the March 2026 core update claims a "data uniqueness" score predicted survival better than domain authority. It recommends shipping "a meaningful default state in the static HTML" with the interactive tool layered on top. — [Appycodes (agency)](https://appycodes.com/blog/programmatic-seo-march-2026-update). A second agency case study claims 3+ unique data points per page and dense internal linking separated winners. — [theStacc (vendor)](https://thestacc.com/blog/programmatic-seo-case-study/). **Treat both as anecdotal.**

### Inferences
- **114 coin pages is modest** and doesn't look like abuse in itself. The risk is that each page currently offers only a 1 to 2 sentence About paragraph, a templated paragraph and JS-only data. In the indexed HTML that looks close to "substantially similar pages".
- Unique value per page that this site can compute at build time and render in HTML:
  - Last snapshot price per exchange and the **cross-exchange spread**. This is the site's genuinely non-commodity angle, because it reads several exchange feeds directly.
  - 24h, 7d, 30d, 90d and 1y % change.
  - 52-week high and low with dates.
  - ATH and date, plus % from ATH.
  - Circulating and max supply, with the source named.
  - A recent daily OHLC/close table (for example the last 30 days).
  - Realized volatility (30-day).
  - Volume share by exchange.
  - Correlation with BTC.
  - Listing or first-data date.
  - "Compare with" links to same-sector coins.
- Create **sector/category hub pages** (Layer 1, L2, DeFi, stablecoins, memecoins) with crawlable `<a href>` lists. These give a "clearly defined, browseable hierarchy", the opposite of the doorway pattern.
- **Don't** create one page per query variant (for example "BTC price in EUR/GBP/…", "BTC to USD", "BTC price prediction") unless each has distinct, useful data. Google names fan-out-variant pages as scaled content abuse.
- **For coins where real data is thin**, such as illiquid tokens, few exchanges or missing feeds, consider merging them into a list page or noindexing them rather than keeping thin standalone pages. Mueller says low-value pages weigh on the site.
- Any AI-written About text should be fact-checked by a human and kept factual. Don't scale it up into long AI essays.

### Gaps
- There are no Google-published numeric thresholds for "enough unique content", and none should be expected.
- I found no independent, non-vendor case study specifically on crypto ticker sites surviving or losing in 2025–2026 core updates.

## 4. Freshness: frequently updated data pages, dateModified/lastmod, and the warning about changing dates without content change

### Takeaway
Google uses `<lastmod>` only if it is "consistently and verifiably accurate". It should reflect "the last significant update" to main content, structured data or links, not trivial changes or the sitemap generation time. Google explicitly lists changing dates "to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed" as a warning sign. Bing likewise wants lastmod to be the true content modification time. Google's typical refresh crawl of a known URL is about 30 days, so a weekly change cycle is faster than Google re-reads most pages.

### Cited Findings
- "Google uses the `<lastmod>` value if it's consistently and verifiably (for example by comparing to the last modification of the page) accurate. The `<lastmod>` value should reflect the date and time of the last significant update to the page. For example, an update to the main content, the structured data, or links on the page is generally considered significant, however an update to the copyright date is not." Google ignores `<priority>` and `<changefreq>`. — [Build and submit a sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap) (updated 2026-07-08)
- Helpful content warning sign: "Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?" — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Bing: "Avoid setting lastmod to the time your sitemap was generated unless the content on that URL was actually updated. The value should reflect the true last modification time of the page content." Lastmod helps Bing "prioritize URLs for recrawling ... or skip them entirely". — [Bing Webmaster blog, July 2025](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2025/7/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search)
- Bing guidelines: "freshness signals like accurate lastmod values and HTTP validation headers such as ETags help Bing detect content changes more reliably." — [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
- Byline dates: use `datePublished`/`dateModified` on a CreativeWork subtype, keep visible and structured dates consistent, and include a timezone. — [Byline dates doc](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates) (updated 2025-12-10)
- Illyes's timing ranges (Search Central Live, reported Oct 2026): discovering a new URL takes about 20 hours typically, "weeks to never" at the slowest. Refreshing a known URL takes about 30 days typically. Title and snippet updates take 1–2 days typically and several weeks or months at the slowest. — [SEJ, Oct 4, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-crawling-indexing-recovery-timing-ranges/591829); [SEOCOM recap](https://seocom.agency/en/blog/how-long-does-google-take-to-index-a-change) (secondary; the slides themselves were not seen)
- Mueller (2018, pre-2025): Googlebot recrawls important pages such as the home page and category pages more often, "maybe every few days". — [SEJ 2018](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-john-mueller-reveals-often-site-re-indexed-search/241427)

### Inferences
- If a scheduled build (for example daily) regenerates each coin page's static snapshot (stats, historical table), the main content really changed. A lastmod equal to that build's data timestamp is then accurate. **Don't** bump lastmod for template-only deploys that change nothing visible. Changes to links or structured data do count.
- To keep lastmod "verifiably accurate", compute it from a hash of the rendered main content. Update lastmod only when the hash changes.
- Show "Price snapshot as of 2026-10-30 00:00 UTC; live prices update in your browser" so the visible and machine dates agree. If you add `dateModified` (on WebPage), set it to the same value.
- The live price changing every second shouldn't be represented as page modification. Googlebot sees only the build snapshot.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no guidance specific to pages whose primary content is real-time data, such as finance tickers, beyond the general lastmod and date rules.
- No official statement says whether WebPage `dateModified` (as opposed to Article) is used. Google's byline doc recommends CreativeWork subtypes; WebPage is a CreativeWork, but its use for byline dates is unconfirmed.

## 5. Discovery for a new site: sitemaps, IndexNow and Crawler Hints, internal links, URL Inspection limits, Bing Webmaster Tools, and legitimate link earning

### Takeaway
For Google, discovery comes from sitemaps (a hint only), crawlable internal links and external links. Request Indexing is a small, unpublished per-property quota and "requesting a recrawl multiple times ... won't get it crawled any faster". IndexNow is for Bing and other participating engines; Cloudflare Crawler Hints automates it from cache MISS events. Links earned from a free tool are legitimate. Google's spam policy explicitly names "keyword-rich, hidden, or low-quality links embedded in widgets that are distributed across various sites" and "requiring a link as part of a Terms of Service" without allowing nofollow.

### Cited Findings
**Sitemaps and recrawl**
- Submitting a sitemap is "merely a hint". Use absolute canonical URLs. Reference the sitemap in robots.txt or submit it via Search Console or its API. — [Build and submit a sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap)
- URL Inspection: "there's a quota for submitting individual URLs and requesting a recrawl multiple times for the same URL won't get it crawled any faster." "Our systems prioritize the fast inclusion of high quality, useful content." — [Ask Google to recrawl](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl)
- Google doesn't publish the manual Request Indexing quota. The URL Inspection API allows 2,000 inspections per day per property but doesn't request indexing. — [Incremys summary](https://www.incremys.com/en/resources/blog/google-search-console-quota) (secondary). A vendor claims "roughly 10–12 manual requests per property per day". — [IndexBolt (vendor; unverified)](https://www.indexbolt.com/blog/request-indexing-google-search-console)
- The Indexing API quota page was updated Oct 2026: 200 publish requests per day by default. The API is officially only for JobPosting/BroadcastEvent pages, so it is **not** for coin pages. — [Indexing API quota](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/indexing-api/v3/quota-pricing); [Incremys](https://www.incremys.com/en/resources/blog/google-search-console-quota)
- Mueller (Oct 2026) on a new site's stalled indexing report: "Indexing doesn't change quickly, and when it does, you see that reflected in the performance reports first." — [SEJ, Oct 27, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-responds-to-concern-over-search-console-indexing-reports/594494)

**IndexNow, Cloudflare and Bing**
- IndexNow lets sites "notify search engines whenever content ... is created, updated, or deleted". Setup: generate a key, host the key file (UTF-8 .txt) at the root, and submit single or bulk URLs. Response codes include 422 (URLs not matching host or key) and 429 (too many requests, "potential Spam"). — [Bing IndexNow get started](https://www.bing.com/indexnow/getstarted)
- IndexNow FAQ: don't submit unchanged URLs repeatedly. IndexNow "is not intended for retroactive submission". Use sitemaps with accurate lastmod for older changes. Indexing is not guaranteed. — [IndexNow FAQ](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)
- Cloudflare Crawler Hints "uses cache-status MISS to determine when content has likely been updated and sends it to IndexNow's crawler". 4xx-or-higher responses are not reported. It is enabled in Caching > Configuration. — [Cloudflare Crawler Hints docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/advanced-configuration/crawler-hints) (updated Aug 14, 2026)
- Bing Webmaster Guidelines: discover URLs via "IndexNow URL submission, XML sitemaps, crawlable internal links, external links". Use IndexNow when URLs are added, updated or removed. Return 404 for removed content. Sitemaps should list only canonical URLs. "Place essential information near the top" and make facts explicit and visible on the URL. Link schemes and "automatically generated content at scale" reduce eligibility. — [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
- Bing Webmaster Tools added an "AI Performance" report (public preview Feb 2026) showing citations in Copilot and Bing AI answers, grounding queries and page-level citations. Intents and Topics were added in June 2026, and "Topics Portfolio (Beta)" in Oct 2026. Topics may be missing for small sites. — [Bing blog Feb 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview); [Bing blog Jun 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/search/June-2026/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare); [Bing blog Oct 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/10/introducing-topics-portfolio-beta-in-ai-performance/)

**Links: legitimate versus spam**
- Link spam examples:
  - buying or selling links
  - excessive link exchanges
  - automated link creation
  - "Requiring a link as part of a Terms of Service, contract, or similar arrangement without allowing a third-party content owner the choice of qualifying the outbound link"
  - "Keyword-rich, hidden, or low-quality links embedded in widgets that are distributed across various sites"
  - "Widely distributed links in the footers or templates of various sites"
  - low-quality directory links
  - "Creating low-value content primarily for the purposes of manipulating linking and ranking signals"
  
  Paid or sponsored links are fine with `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`. — [Spam policies: link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Google's AI guide says to ignore "pursuing inauthentic mentions". — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- Internal links: anchor text should be "descriptive, reasonably concise, and relevant". Don't cram keywords into it. — [Link best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable)

### Inferences
- **IndexNow on Cloudflare Pages**: Crawler Hints keys off cache MISS on a proxied zone. It's unclear how reliably that fires for Pages static assets after a deploy (see Gaps). The more deterministic option is a post-deploy step that POSTs the changed URLs (diff of content hashes) to `api.indexnow.org` in one batch, with the key file shipped in the build output. Submit only URLs whose content changed. Don't resubmit all 114 every build.
- **Google discovery**: submit sitemap.xml in Search Console. Make sure every coin page is reachable through static `<a href>` from the home page and from sector hubs within about 2 clicks. Use Request Indexing sparingly: home page, methodology page, hubs. Expect a new low-link site to wait days to weeks.
- **Legitimate link-earning for a free tool:**
  1. **Embeddable price widget.** Make the attribution a visible brand-name link ("Prices by CryptoPriceTicker") to the home page or the specific coin page. Don't use keyword anchors, hidden links or multiple links. Don't make the link a condition of use; let embedders nofollow it, or ship it nofollow by default. That stays clearly within the widget and ToS rules.
  2. **Free public data endpoint or CSV downloads** (for example a historical daily close CSV per coin) with a documented license and attribution request. People link to data sources.
  3. **Original data posts.** Periodic, genuinely computed stats such as cross-exchange spread reports or volatility rankings that journalists and Reddit/forum users can cite.
  4. **Listings** in relevant curated resource lists, open-source "awesome" lists, and Product Hunt/Hacker News launches, as honest submissions (not low-quality directories).
  5. Answering questions in communities where a link genuinely helps, without stuffing links.
- **Avoid**: buying links, PBNs, link exchanges, footer links on other sites, mass directories, paid guest posts with followed links, and "AI mention" campaigns.

### Gaps
- I found no Cloudflare doc confirming whether Crawler Hints fires for Cloudflare Pages (as opposed to proxied-origin) responses.
- I didn't fetch the current list of IndexNow participating engines. From prior knowledge: Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver and Yep participate, and Google does not. This was not re-verified in this session.
- The manual Request Indexing quota is unpublished. The only numbers found came from vendors.

## 6. Competitive landscape: how big price sites structure coin pages (structure only)

### Takeaway
Major coin pages use the same skeleton:
- title: "<Coin> price today / live price, chart, market cap"
- above the fold: price, change and chart with time ranges
- stats block: market cap, supply, volume, rank, 24h high/low, ATH
- converter
- historical price section or table
- about/what-is text
- markets/exchanges list
- FAQ and disclaimer

The structure is a commodity. Differentiation must come from data or angle.

### Cited Findings
- **Kraken** `/prices/bitcoin`:
  - Title: "Bitcoin price today - BTC price chart & live trends".
  - In-page nav: "Price today, BTC Price history, About BTC, Categories, DCA Tool, FAQs".
  - Chart ranges: 1H, 24H, 1W, 1M, 1Y, 5Y.
  - Market stats: market cap, circulating supply, volume, market rank, 24H high/low/change, ATH.
  - Prose: "Bitcoin price today is $X".
  - An "all time high" block (ATH price, ATH date, days since, % difference from ATH).
  - A "historical price table" by day/week/month/year (High/Low/Open/Close/% change).
  - 1-year high/low, all-time range, fully diluted cap, listed date.
  - Bull/bear takes, FAQ, investment disclaimer.
  
  — [Kraken BTC price page](https://www.kraken.com/prices/bitcoin)
- **CoinMarketCap** `/currencies/bitcoin/`:
  - Title: "Bitcoin price today, BTC to USD live price, marketcap and chart | CoinMarketCap".
  - Sections: BTC to USD converter, price performance, "See historical data" link (a separate `/historical-data/` page), tags, markets table (CEX/DEX, Spot/Perpetual/Futures), long "about" text, related pages.
  - Summary prose: "The live Bitcoin price today is $X USD with a 24-hour trading volume of … ranking is #1 … circulating supply … max supply".
  - Affiliate disclaimer.
  
  — [CoinMarketCap BTC](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/bitcoin)
- **CoinGecko** `/en/coins/bitcoin`:
  - Title: "Bitcoin Price: BTC/USD Live Price Chart, Market Cap & News Today | CoinGecko".
  - Sections: converter, statistics (market cap, market cap/FDV, FDV, 24h volume, perps open interest, funding rate), historical price (24h range, 7d range, ATH, ATL with %), markets list, FAQ (for example "What is the market cap of Bitcoin (BTC)?"), news, prediction-market odds, AI Q&A.
  - Summary prose: "The price of Bitcoin (BTC) is $X today with a 24-hour trading volume of … This represents a -0.90% price decline in the last 24 hours and a -3.20% price decline in the past 7 days."
  
  — [CoinGecko BTC](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin)
- Kraken also has separate `/convert/btc` pages ("Trade Bitcoin") with BTC→USD amount tables, which shows converter intent is often split onto its own URL. — [Kraken convert](https://www.kraken.com/convert/btc)

### Inferences
- The common title vocabulary is "price today", "live price", "chart", "market cap" and "BTC to USD". The current title ("Bitcoin (BTC) Live Price & Chart | CryptoPriceTicker.com") already has the core terms. A test variant could add "Today" and/or "USD", for example "Bitcoin (BTC) Price Today – Live USD Chart | CryptoPriceTicker". Test it rather than switching all pages at once.
- Big sites put a one-sentence, data-filled summary near the top ("The price of X is $Y today … down Z% in 24h"). A build-time version of this sentence gives Google an indexable, page-specific summary and a candidate snippet. It must carry a timestamp because it goes stale.
- The site can't out-cover CMC or CoinGecko. Its plausible niche is **direct multi-exchange live prices with visible spread and latency**, plus fast, light pages. Lead with that on the page and in meta descriptions.

### Gaps
- I did not observe Coinbase's price page structure in this session.
- I can't see which elements drive these sites' rankings; their brand authority dominates.
- For head terms like "bitcoin price", Google typically shows its own finance/price module above organic results. That reduces clicks for every site, but I found no 2025–2026 source quantifying it for crypto queries.

## 7. Measuring and iterating: Search Console metrics and heuristics for title/meta tests on a small site (and the "self-improving" weekly AI loop)

### Takeaway
On a small site, Search Console data is sparse and partly anonymized. Query groups and the branded filter may not be available at low volume. Google's own timings say title changes take days to weeks to show and known URLs are recrawled about every 30 days. AI-edited metadata must be fact-checked under Google's AI guidance. CTR is structurally depressed by AI Overviews (about -58% at position 1 in Ahrefs' Dec 2025 data). So a weekly title-rewrite loop will mostly chase noise. Use page-group tests over 4+ week windows with guardrails.

### Cited Findings
- Search Console metrics: clicks, impressions, CTR and average position (of the topmost result). — [Performance report help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553)
- Anonymized queries are "those that aren't issued by more than a few dozen users over a two-to-three month period". They're omitted from tables but included in chart totals (pre-2025 post, still the documented behavior). — [GSC data deep dive](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/10/performance-data-deep-dive)
- Google suggests using the query dimension "to find queries with high impressions and low CTR for opportunities to improve your page title and description." — [Performance report dimensions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17011259)
- Query groups (Oct 2025, in Insights) are "available only to properties that have a large volume of queries". — [Google blog Oct 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/10/search-console-query-groups)
- Branded queries filter (Nov 2025, available to all eligible sites from Mar 11, 2026): "This filter isn't available for sites with a low number of impressions." — [Google blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/search-console-branded-filter); [Dimensions help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17011259)
- AI-generated metadata must be "manually factcheck[ed]". The guidance on reviewing AI content "also applies to metadata like `<title>` elements, meta description elements, structured data". — [Using gen AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- Google's A/B testing doc: run experiments "only as long as necessary". Use `rel=canonical` rather than noindex for URL-variant tests. Don't cloak. — [A/B testing best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/website-testing)
- Title link changes need recrawl and reprocessing, which takes "a few days to a few weeks". — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link). Typical title and snippet update is 1–2 days, slowest several weeks or months. Known-URL refresh is about 30 days. — [SEJ on Illyes](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-crawling-indexing-recovery-timing-ranges/591829)
- AI Overviews and CTR:
  - Ahrefs (Feb 2026, aggregated GSC data, Dec 2025): AI Overviews reduce position-1 CTR by 58% (position 2: -50.8%; position 10: -19.4%). Position-1 CTR for informational keywords fell from 0.076 (Dec 2023) to 0.039 (Dec 2025). — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)
  - Seer, SISTRIX (Germany, -59% at position 1) and Pew report similar declines. — [SEJ summary, Apr 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overview-ctr-fell-61-but-clicks-didnt-collapse/572993)
  - Seer notes impression surges can come from Google's own SERP changes, and advises analyzing months separately. — [same](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overview-ctr-fell-61-but-clicks-didnt-collapse/572993)
- Google says sites appearing in AI features see "higher quality" clicks (Google's claim, not independently verified). — [Google blog May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search)
- Google warns: "Be wary of third-party tools that promise ranking success or claim to use 'internal' Google metrics." — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

### Inferences (design for the "self-improving" loop)
- **Cadence**: run the agent weekly to *analyze and propose*. *Ship* changes at most every 4 weeks per page group, and evaluate on 28-day windows against the previous 28 days and the same period of a control group.
- **Unit of testing**: the template or page group, not single pages. For example, split the 114 coins into two matched halves by impressions and rank. Apply a title variant to half and keep the other half as control. Compare CTR change in the test group against the control group (difference-in-differences). Only judge groups with at least about 1,000 impressions per window (a heuristic, not a Google figure). Compare CTR within similar position buckets (1–3, 4–10, 11–20), because CTR moves with position.
- **Guardrails for the agent**:
  - Never put live prices in titles.
  - Never stuff keywords or repeat the site name.
  - Never change dates or lastmod without real content change.
  - Never add pages for fan-out query variants.
  - Every generated title, meta and About text goes through a factual check against the page's own data and must stay human-readable.
  - Log every change (git) so performance shifts can be attributed.
  - Use Search Console annotations, which exist per the help sidebar (see Gaps).
  - Don't edit anything within about 2 weeks of a confirmed core or spam update starting or ending, because the data is confounded.
- **Better use of the loop**: use it to find *content gaps* rather than rewrite titles weekly. Queries with impressions but no matching section (for example "bitcoin all time high", "eth supply") show which computed data blocks to add to the template. That improves all 114 pages with real data.
- **Other data sources**: Search Console Generative AI performance report (AI Overview and AI Mode impressions by page), Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance (citations and grounding queries) and Bing's own performance data.

### Gaps
- There is no official Google guidance on minimum sample sizes for SEO title tests. The thresholds above are heuristics.
- I didn't fetch the Search Console annotations help page (it appears in the help sidebar), so I can't describe its capabilities.
- I found no data on CTR for crypto price queries specifically, or on whether price queries usually trigger AI Overviews versus Google's finance module.

## 8. Prioritized, implementable checklist (synthesized from the findings above)

### Takeaway
The highest-leverage changes come first. Make each coin page's indexable HTML carry unique, build-time computed data plus clear provenance. Add trust pages: About, Methodology, Contact. Fix discovery: hubs, sitemap lastmod, IndexNow. Then earn links honestly through the widget and data. Run the AI loop as a slow, guarded, group-level experiment. Don't chase AI-specific hacks.

### Cited Findings
Each item links to the evidence behind it. P0 = do now, P1 = next, P2 = later.

**(a) On-page and template changes (code)**
1. **P0. Render a build-time snapshot of all key data in static HTML on every coin page**, then let JS update it live:
   - price per exchange and cross-exchange spread
   - 24h, 7d, 30d and 1y % change
   - 52-week high/low with dates
   - ATH, ATH date and % from ATH
   - circulating/max supply with source
   - last-30-day daily close table
   - 30-day volatility

   Basis: JS rendering is queued and pre-rendering is still recommended ([JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)). Value must come from original data or effort ([Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content); [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Big sites show these blocks ([Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/prices/bitcoin), [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin)).
2. **P0. Add a one-sentence, data-filled summary near the top** in HTML ("Bitcoin (BTC) was $X at HH:MM UTC on DATE, Y% over 24h, Z% below its ATH of $A set on D"), with a visible "as of" timestamp. Mirrors [CMC](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/bitcoin)/[CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin). Gives Google a page-specific snippet candidate ([Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)).
3. **P0. Make sure all navigation is `<a href>`** (coin list, related coins, hubs, breadcrumbs) in the initial HTML. Keep the canonical in HTML and never change it with JS. Never ship `noindex` in HTML expecting JS to remove it. — [Links crawlable](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable); [JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)
4. **P0. Create About, Methodology/Data Sources and Contact pages.** Link Methodology from each coin page's "Where this price comes from" paragraph. Add a "not investment advice" disclaimer and Organization JSON-LD pointing to About. Don't use fake author personas. — [QRG](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf); [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content); [CMC methodology model](https://support.coinmarketcap.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015968632-How-are-prices-calculated-on-CoinMarketCap)
5. **P0. Make lastmod honest.** Set it from a content hash of each page's main content, structured data and links. Change it only when that hash changes. Keep any visible "updated" date and any `dateModified` identical. — [Sitemaps](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap); [Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2025/7/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search)
6. **P1. Add sector/category hub pages** (L1, L2, DeFi, stablecoins and so on) with tables built from the same data, plus "compare with peers" links on coin pages, to form a browseable hierarchy. — [Doorway policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
7. **P1. Titles**: keep the pattern (short, coin + ticker + intent + brand). No live prices. Make the `<h1>` match the title's main part. Optionally test "Price Today"/"USD" variants by page group. — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
8. **P1. Meta descriptions**: generate them from page-specific data in readable sentences, for example "Live BTC price from Coinbase, Kraken and Binance with cross-exchange spread, 52-week range and ATH." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)
9. **P1. Core Web Vitals**:
   - Throttle and batch WebSocket DOM writes (requestAnimationFrame) for INP <200ms.
   - Reserve chart space and use tabular-nums with fixed widths for CLS <0.1.
   - Inline critical CSS and defer the chart library for LCP ≤2.5s.
   - Fingerprint JS filenames.

   — [Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals); [JS SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)
10. **P1. Structured data**: keep WebSite (site name), Organization, BreadcrumbList and WebPage. Make sure all of it matches visible content. Don't expect FAQ rich results (gone since May 7, 2026). Optional: Dataset markup on a historical-data download page, which is used only by Dataset Search. — [Updates log](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [SEJ on Dataset](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-deprecates-practice-problem-structured-data-in-search/560076); [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
11. **P2. Thin coins**: for coins with too little real data (one exchange, missing supply), fold them into a list page or noindex them rather than keep thin standalone pages. — [Mueller via SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-old-low-value-pages-may-affect-site-recovery/588837)
12. **P2. Downloadable per-coin historical CSV or a small public JSON endpoint** with an attribution request. This creates a linkable asset.

**(b) Off-page and owner actions**
1. **P0.** Verify the domain property in Google Search Console and submit sitemap.xml. Check that "Search generative AI" is set to Include (the default). Use URL Inspection on the home page, a coin page and Methodology to confirm the rendered HTML contains the static data and links. Use Request Indexing only for a handful of key URLs. — [Recrawl](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl); [Gen AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024)
2. **P0.** Verify in Bing Webmaster Tools (it can import from Search Console) and submit the sitemap. Set up IndexNow: ship the key file and add a post-deploy script that submits only changed URLs. Optionally enable Cloudflare Crawler Hints. Monitor Bing AI Performance. — [Bing guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a); [IndexNow](https://www.bing.com/indexnow/getstarted); [Crawler Hints](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/advanced-configuration/crawler-hints)
3. **P1.** Launch the embeddable widget. The attribution link is visible, brand-name only, and not required by the terms; let embedders nofollow it or default it to nofollow. — [Link spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
4. **P1.** Publish periodic original data notes (for example monthly cross-exchange spread or volatility stats) and share them where relevant: communities, journalists, curated lists, Show HN or Product Hunt.
5. **P2.** Track annotations of deploys against Search Console data, and avoid changes during core or spam update rollouts ([status dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)).

**(c) Things to avoid**
- llms.txt, "AI-only" markup, content "chunking" or rewriting for AI as a Google tactic. Google ignores these. — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- Generating pages for query variants or fan-out queries (for example per-fiat, "price prediction" or "to USD" clones) without distinct data. That is scaled content abuse. — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Long AI-written "About" essays at scale, or unreviewed AI titles and metas. — [Using gen AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content); [QRG §4.6.5–4.6.6](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Bumping dates or lastmod without substantive change. — [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Live prices in `<title>`, keyword-stuffed titles, and blocks of numbers or keywords. — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link); [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Fake authors, credentials or AI headshots. — [QRG](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Buying links, link exchanges, keyword-anchored widget links, mandatory-link ToS, footer links on other sites, low-quality directories, "inauthentic mentions". — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Showing Googlebot different content from users (cloaking). For example, don't serve a data-rich page only to bots. — [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [Bing guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
- FAQPage markup added for rich results (deprecated) or structured data that doesn't match visible text. — [Updates log](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- Weekly sitewide title churn judged on 7-day data. — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link); [Illyes timings via SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-crawling-indexing-recovery-timing-ranges/591829)
- Spamming Request Indexing or IndexNow with unchanged URLs. — [Recrawl](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl); [IndexNow FAQ](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)

### Inferences
- Priority logic: P0 items fix indexable thinness and trust, the two most likely reasons a young YMYL-adjacent templated site stays unindexed or unranked. P1 items improve CTR, crawl paths and links. P2 items are refinements.
- Realistic expectations: with no links and Google's indexing timings, meaningful non-branded Google traffic for head terms like "bitcoin price" is unlikely near-term. Long-tail data queries ("X all time high", "X 52 week high", "X price on Coinbase vs Kraken") and Bing/Copilot are more reachable. This is an inference, not a sourced forecast.

### Gaps
- None of the checklist items has published causal evidence specific to crypto ticker sites. The recommendations are derived from Google and Bing guidance plus competitor observation.
- Whether the WebSocket-driven live table harms INP in practice depends on the implementation. Measure it with field data (CrUX/Search Console CWV report, once traffic is enough) or lab tools.
